# Student Handbook: B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering

Pathwise Institute of Technology is fictional. This handbook is demo data for the Pathwise advisor.

## 1. Program overview

The B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering runs for four years, each split into two semesters. Years 1 and 2 are all core courses. From Year 3, students add electives and may earn a specialization by focusing their electives on one track. Every course in the catalog belongs to one year and one semester and is offered only in that semester.

## 2. Credit load

### 2.1 Normal load

A normal semester load is between 9 and 20 credits. Students may not register for more than 20 credits in a semester without an overload approval.

### 2.2 Overload

Students with a CGPA of 8.0 or higher may request an overload of up to 24 credits in one semester. An overload needs written approval from the faculty advisor before registration closes. Students below a CGPA of 8.0 cannot overload.

### 2.3 Underload

Registering for fewer than 9 credits needs approval from the head of department, usually for medical or personal reasons, and may delay graduation.

## 3. Grading

### 3.1 Grades and grade points

Courses are graded O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7), B (6), C (5), P (4), and F (0). A grade of P or better is a pass. F is a fail and earns no credits.

### 3.2 CGPA

The cumulative grade point average (CGPA) is the credit-weighted average of grade points over all graded courses taken so far, including failed attempts until they are replaced by a pass.

### 3.3 Failing and repeating a course

A failed core course must be repeated the next time it is offered, which is the same semester of the following year. When a failed course is passed on a later attempt, the new grade replaces the F in the CGPA. A course that was passed cannot be repeated to improve the grade.

## 4. Prerequisites

### 4.1 The rule

A student may register for a course only after passing every prerequisite listed for it in the catalog, with a grade of P or better. Courses in progress do not count as passed. Some courses also require a minimum number of earned credits, shown in the catalog.

### 4.2 Waivers

A prerequisite can be waived only by the instructor of the course together with the head of department, and only for students who show equivalent preparation. Waivers are rare and are never granted for Capstone Project II.

## 5. Electives and specializations

### 5.1 Elective requirement

Every student must pass at least four elective courses totaling at least 13 credits. Electives are offered in Year 3 Semester 2 and in Year 4.

### 5.2 Specialization tracks

Passing at least three electives from the same track earns a specialization noted on the transcript. The tracks are AI and Machine Learning, Data Science, and Systems. Some electives count toward two tracks; each elective counts once toward the elective requirement but may count toward every track it belongs to.

### 5.3 Industry internship

The Industry Internship (CS 499) counts as an elective but belongs to no track, so it does not count toward a specialization. It needs at least 70 earned credits and an approved company placement of at least 12 weeks.

## 6. Registration

### 6.1 Timeline

Registration for the next semester opens four weeks before the current semester ends and closes one week after the next semester starts.

### 6.2 Timetable clashes

Each course meets in one timetable slot. A student cannot register for two courses that meet in the same slot in the same semester. When two wanted electives clash, the student must choose one; the other may be offered again the next year.

### 6.3 Adding and dropping

Courses can be added or dropped without penalty during the first two weeks of the semester. After that, a dropped course appears on the transcript as W (withdrawn) and earns no credits but does not affect the CGPA.

## 7. Capstone project

The capstone is a year-long team project in Year 4. Capstone Project I (CS 491) needs Software Engineering (CS 360) and at least 70 earned credits. Capstone Project II (CS 492) needs a pass in Capstone Project I in the same academic year.

## 8. Graduation requirements

To graduate, a student must pass every core course in the catalog (71 credits), meet the elective requirement in section 5.1, earn at least 86 credits in total, and finish with a CGPA of at least 5.0.

## 9. Academic standing

A student whose CGPA falls below 5.0 at the end of a semester is placed on academic probation. A student on probation may register for at most 16 credits and must meet the faculty advisor before registering. Probation ends when the CGPA is 5.0 or higher at the end of a semester.

## 10. Advising

Every student has a faculty advisor. The advisor approves overloads, discusses elective choices, and signs off on the registration of students on probation. The Pathwise advisor tool gives guidance from this handbook and the catalog only; final approval always rests with the faculty advisor.
